# 11 — Drums and Percussion

The drums define **tempo, feel, and energy** — and, more than any other instrument, the
**style**. A pop song and a punk song can share chords; they will never share a drum part.

## 11.1 The drum kit and its roles

| Piece | GM MIDI note | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Kick (bass drum)** | 36 (C2), 35 | Low-end pulse, locks with bass; anchors downbeats |
| **Snare** | 38 (D2), 40 (electric) | The **backbeat** (2 & 4); ghost notes for groove |
| Side stick / rim click | 37 | Quiet verses, ballads, bossa |
| Clap | 39 | Pop/dance backbeat, layered with snare |
| **Closed hi-hat** | 42 (F♯2) | Time-keeping subdivision (8ths or 16ths) |
| Pedal hi-hat | 44 | Foot "chick", keeps time when hands are elsewhere |
| **Open hi-hat** | 46 | Accents, lift, "wash" (disco offbeats) |
| **Ride cymbal** | 51 (bell 53) | Time-keeping alternative to hi-hat; choruses, jazz |
| **Crash cymbal** | 49, 57 | Marks section starts / big accents (beat 1 after a fill) |
| China / splash | 52 / 55 | Effects, metal accents |
| High / mid tom | 50, 48, 47, 45 | Fills, tribal grooves |
| Floor tom | 43, 41 | Fills, heavy tom grooves ("Sing, Sing, Sing", stomp/tribal beats) |
| Tambourine | 54 | Adds 8th/16th shimmer or backbeat in choruses (pop, Motown) |
| Shaker / cabasa | 70 / 69 | 16th-note motion, soft layer |
| Cowbell | 56 | Funk, Latin, "more cowbell" |
| Congas / bongos | 60–64 | Latin, funk, percussion layers |

**Primary trio**: kick (low), snare (mid), hi-hat/ride (high). Most grooves are defined by
how these three interact.

## 11.2 Core grooves (16-step grid, one bar of 4/4)

Notation: `x` = hit, `X` = accent, `o` = open hi-hat, `g` = ghost note, `.` = rest.
Steps: `1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a`.

### Basic rock beat (8th-note)
```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Hi-hat:  x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare:   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Kick:    x . . . . . . . x . x . . . . .
```
The foundation of rock and pop-rock. Kick on 1 and 3 (with variations like the "& of 3"),
snare on 2 and 4.

### Pop/rock with 16th hats (driving)
```
Hi-hat:  x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x
Snare:   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Kick:    x . . x . . x . x . . . . . x .
```

### Four-on-the-floor (disco / dance-pop / house)
```
Hi-hat:  . . o . . . o . . . o . . . o .     (open hat on offbeats)
Snare:   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .     (or clap)
Kick:    x . . . x . . . x . . . x . . .
```

### Half-time (heavy choruses, trap, post-rock)
```
Hi-hat:  x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare:   . . . . . . . . X . . . . . . .     (snare only on 3)
Kick:    x . . . . . x . . . . . . . . .
```

### Punk / double-time ("skank" or "d-beat"-like)
```
Hi-hat:  x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .     (often on crash/ride instead)
Snare:   . . x . . . x . . . x . . . x .     (snare on every "&")
Kick:    x . . . x . . . x . . . x . . .
```

### Shuffle (12/8 or swung 8ths) — blues, boogie
```
Triplet grid: 1 trip let 2 trip let 3 trip let 4 trip let
Hi-hat/ride:  x  .   x   x  .   x   x  .   x   x  .   x
Snare:        .  .   .   X  .   .   .  .   .   X  .   .
Kick:         x  .   .   .  .   .   x  .   .   .  .   .
```

### Funk (16th, ghost notes)
```
Hi-hat:  x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x
Snare:   . . . g X . . g . g . . X . . g
Kick:    x . . x . . . . . . x . . x . .
```
Ghost notes (very soft snare hits) give funk its "grease". "The one" (beat 1) is heavily
emphasised in James Brown–style funk.

### Motown / soul
Snare (often with tambourine) on **every quarter** or on 2 & 4 with a strong tambourine
backbeat; kick on quarters or 1 and 3 with pickups.

### Reggae one-drop
```
Hi-hat:  x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .     (often swung)
Snare:   . . . . . . . . X . . . . . . .     (cross-stick on 3)
Kick:    . . . . . . . . x . . . . . . .     (kick only on 3 — "drop" on the one)
```

### Metal double-kick
Kick in continuous 16ths (or 8th-note triplets), snare on 2 & 4 (or blast beats:
alternating kick/snare as fast as possible in extreme metal).

### Trap / modern pop (half-time feel)
Snare/clap on beat 3 (half-time), syncopated 808 kicks, **hi-hat rolls** (32nd/triplet
bursts) for rhythmic interest.

## 11.3 Fills

- Placed at the end of 4/8/16-bar phrases and before section changes (chapter 03).
- Usually played on snare and toms, descending in pitch, ending with **crash + kick on 1**.
- Fill length scales with the importance of the transition:
  - Within a section, every 4 bars: a small variation (extra kick, open hat, 1-beat fill).
  - End of 8 bars: 1–2 beat fill.
  - Into the chorus: full-bar fill, or a **snare roll/build** (8ths → 16ths → 32nds) in
    pop/EDM.
- **Stops before the downbeat** (silence on beat 4) are a powerful alternative to fills.

Common fill shapes (last bar, 16 steps):
```
1-beat:  . . . . . . . . . . . . S S T F       (S=snare, T=tom, F=floor tom)
2-beat:  . . . . . . . . S S S S T T F F
Full:    S S S S T T T T t t t t F F F F       (high→low toms)
Build:   S . S . S . S . S S S S S S S S       (snare build into chorus)
```

## 11.4 Dynamics and feel — what makes drums "human"

### Velocity (loudness)
- **Accents**: backbeat snare loud (~100–120 MIDI velocity); hi-hats lower (~60–90), with
  **downbeats louder than offbeats** (e.g. 90 on beats, 70 on "&", 55 on "e/a").
- **Ghost notes**: snare at ~20–45.
- Crashes loud on section starts.
- Verses use lighter playing (or side stick, closed hats); choruses open up (ride, open
  hats, crash on 1, louder snare).

### Timing (micro-timing)
- Real drummers are not perfectly quantised. Small deviations (±5–15 ms) feel human.
- **"Behind the beat"** (snare slightly late, ~10–25 ms) = laid back (soul, hip-hop,
  some rock).
- **"On top / ahead"** = urgent, driving (punk, some pop-punk).
- **Swing** applied to 8ths or 16ths (chapter 03).
- Hi-hat placement and velocity variation contribute most to "feel".

### Dynamic shaping across the song
- Energy is raised by: denser hat subdivision (8ths → 16ths), hats → ride/crash, adding
  kick notes, adding percussion layers (tambourine, shakers), louder snare, more fills.
- Energy is lowered by: side-stick instead of snare, closed hats or no hats, kick only,
  half-time, or removing drums entirely (breakdown, intro).
- **Builds**: snare rolls accelerating, rising tom patterns, crescendos, removing kick before
  the drop.

### Kit sound per style (production)
- Classic rock: big room, natural kit, strong snare crack.
- 80s pop/rock: gated reverb snare, big toms.
- Punk: fast, bright, loose.
- Metal: tight, clicky kick, triggered/sampled drums.
- Modern pop: programmed/sampled, layered claps, 808 kick, heavy compression and sidechain.
- Indie: dry, vintage, sometimes lo-fi.

## 11.5 Percussion layers

Percussion (tambourine, shakers, congas, claps) fills the **high-frequency rhythmic space**
and adds motion without much volume. Typical uses:

- **Tambourine on 2 & 4 or 8ths** in choruses — instant energy lift (Motown, Britpop).
- **Shaker in 16ths** throughout — gentle motion in acoustic pop and ballads.
- **Hand claps** on the backbeat — pop, gospel, stomp-clap folk-pop.
- **Congas/bongos** — Latin rock (Santana), funk, disco.

- Build a **pattern library** per style × energy level (e.g. verse_low, verse_high,
  chorus, half_time, fill_1beat, fill_full).
- Generate per bar: pick the pattern for the section's energy; on phrase-end bars, replace
  the last 1–4 beats with a fill; add a crash on 1 of each new section.
- Humanise: velocity jitter ±5–10, timing jitter ±5–10 ms, style-specific swing and
  "behind the beat" offsets on the snare.
- Use the **kick pattern as an input to the bass generator** (chapter 12).
