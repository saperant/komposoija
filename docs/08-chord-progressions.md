# 08 — Chord Progressions

A **chord progression** is a sequence of chords, usually repeated in a loop of 2, 4 or 8
bars. In pop/rock most chords last **one bar** or **half a bar**; changes usually happen on
beat 1 (often **anticipated** on the "& of 4").

## 8.1 The workhorse progressions

Examples in C major / A minor. Roman numerals make them transposable.

| Name | Numerals | In C | Famous examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Axis" / pop-punk** | I–V–vi–IV | C–G–Am–F | "Let It Be", "With or Without You", "No Woman No Cry", "Someone Like You", "Don't Stop Believin'" (verse) |
| **"Sensitive female" / vi start** | vi–IV–I–V | Am–F–C–G | "Zombie", "Numb", "Poker Face", "Save Tonight" |
| **50s / doo-wop** | I–vi–IV–V | C–Am–F–G | "Stand by Me", "Every Breath You Take", "Earth Angel", "Unchained Melody" |
| **Three-chord rock** | I–IV–V | C–F–G | "La Bamba", "Twist and Shout", "Wild Thing", countless punk |
| **I–IV loop** | I–IV | C–F | "Born in the U.S.A.", many gospel and anthem vamps |
| **Mixolydian rock** | I–♭VII–IV–I | C–B♭–F–C | "Hey Jude" coda, "Sweet Home Alabama" (as V–IV–I), "Takin' Care of Business" |
| **Aeolian rock** | i–♭VI–♭VII | Am–F–G | "All Along the Watchtower" (i–♭VII–♭VI), "Stairway" end, "Livin' on a Prayer" chorus |
| **Epic minor** | i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII | Am–F–C–G | Same chords as the axis, minor rotation: "Apologize", film/trailer pop |
| **ii–V–I** | ii–V–I | Dm–G–C | Jazz standard, soul, Beatles bridges |
| **Circle / descending fifths** | vi–ii–V–I | Am–Dm–G–C | "Fly Me to the Moon", "rhythm changes" (I–vi–ii–V), 60s pop |
| **Andalusian** | i–♭VII–♭VI–V | Am–G–F–E | "Hit the Road Jack", "Sultans of Swing", "Runaway" (Del Shannon), "Happy Together" verse |
| **Pachelbel/Canon** | I–V–vi–iii–IV–I–IV–V | C–G–Am–Em–F–C–F–G | "Basket Case", "Graduation", "Go West", "Memories" (Maroon 5) |
| **Royal Road (J-pop)** | IV–V–iii–vi | F–G–Em–Am | Dominant in J-pop, anime themes |
| **12-bar blues** | see 8.4 | | Rock'n'roll, blues, early rock |
| **Plagal loop** | I–IV–I | C–F–C | Gospel "Amen", Oasis-style anthems |
| **Lydian lift** | I–II | C–D | "Dreams" (Fleetwood Mac: F–G), "Man on the Moon" verse |
| **Minor plagal** | I–iv | C–Fm | "Creep" (I–III–IV–iv), "In My Life", "Nowhere Man" emotional dips |

The four chords **I, V, vi, IV** in some order cover an enormous fraction of Western pop
choruses. Rotations of the same loop have different emotional weight: starting on I sounds
confident, starting on vi sounds melancholic.

## 8.2 Cadences — how phrases end

| Cadence | Motion | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authentic (perfect)** | V → I | Full stop, resolved | End of chorus / song |
| **Plagal** | IV → I | "Amen", soft resolution | Rock and gospel endings |
| **Half** | → V | Comma, question | End of verse/pre-chorus, setting up the chorus |
| **Deceptive** | V → vi | Surprise, extends phrase | Delay the chorus resolution; ballads |
| **Rock / "Aeolian"** | ♭VI → ♭VII → I | Heroic, triumphant | "Mario cadence"; arena rock, power metal |
| **Backdoor** | ♭VII → I (or iv–♭VII–I) | Soft, soulful | Soul, Beatles, R&B |
| **Minor plagal** | iv → I | Bittersweet, nostalgic | Ballads, Radiohead, Beatles |

A **pre-chorus** typically ends on **IV or V** (half cadence) so the **chorus downbeat**
lands with a sense of arrival on I or vi.

## 8.3 Borrowed chords (modal mixture)

Take chords from the **parallel minor** (or another mode) while staying in a major key.
In C major, borrowed from C minor / C Mixolydian:

| Chord | Borrowed from | In C | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **♭VII** | Mixolydian/minor | B♭ | The quintessential rock chord |
| **♭VI** | Minor | A♭ | Big, cinematic ("♭VI–♭VII–I") |
| **♭III** | Minor | E♭ | Bluesy, heavy ("Creep" uses III major instead — a secondary-dominant colour) |
| **iv** | Minor | Fm | Melancholy, nostalgic |
| **ii°/iiø** | Minor | Dø | Darker pre-dominant |
| **v** | Mixolydian | Gm | Soft, modal |

In minor keys the reverse is common: **major IV** (Dorian) and **major V** (harmonic minor),
and the **Picardy third** (ending a minor song on a major I).

## 8.4 The 12-bar blues

The foundation of rock'n'roll, blues, R&B and much early rock. In A (all chords usually
dominant 7ths):

```
| A7  | A7  | A7  | A7  |
| D7  | D7  | A7  | A7  |
| E7  | D7  | A7  | E7  |      ← last bar = turnaround to repeat
```

Variants:
- **Quick change** — IV in bar 2: `| A7 | D7 | A7 | A7 |`
- **Turnaround** — last 2 bars `| A7 D7 | A7 E7 |` or with chromatic walk-downs.
- **8-bar** and **16-bar** blues also exist ("Heartbreak Hotel" is an 8-bar blues).
- Lyric form: **AAB** — line, repeat, answer line.

## 8.5 Harmonic rhythm

**Harmonic rhythm** = how often chords change.

- Slow (one chord per 2–4 bars): spacious, modal, groove-based (funk, EDM, drone rock).
- Medium (one per bar): standard pop/rock verse.
- Fast (two per bar or more): energetic or sophisticated (Beatles, jazz, show tunes).

Contrast between sections is a powerful tool: e.g. verse = one chord per 2 bars, chorus =
one chord per bar ⇒ chorus feels like it "moves" more. Pre-choruses often speed up harmonic
rhythm to build tension.

## 8.6 Other useful devices

- **Pedal point** — bass holds one note while chords change above it (`C – F/C – G/C – C`).
  Creates tension and a "building" feeling.
- **Line clichés** — one voice moves chromatically inside a static chord:
  `Am – Am(maj7) – Am7 – Am6` ("Stairway", "My Funny Valentine", "Michelle").
- **Chromatic passing chords** — e.g. C – C♯° – Dm, or B♭ – B° – C.
- **Modulation by step** — the final-chorus key change (see chapter 05).
- **Vamp** — one or two chords repeated indefinitely (funk, Mixolydian rock, dance music).

## 8.7 Writing good progressions — heuristics

1. **Start and/or end on the tonic** (or vi in minor-flavoured pop) to establish the key.
2. **Strong root motion** = down a 5th (V→I), up a 4th, down a 3rd (I→vi→IV). Root motion by
   step (IV→V, ♭VI→♭VII) is energetic; by 2nd downward (I→♭VII) is classic rock.
3. **Put V or IV at phrase ends** to propel into the next phrase.
4. **Repetition is good**: a 4-chord loop repeated is normal for pop.
5. **Vary between sections**: different starting chord, different harmonic rhythm, or a
   borrowed chord in the bridge.
6. **Bridges** often start on a chord not used before (IV, vi, ♭VI, or iii) and end on V.
