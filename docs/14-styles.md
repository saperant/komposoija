# 14 — Musical Styles and Their Essential Features

Each style below lists the features that matter most if you want to **recognise** it or
**generate** it: tempo, meter/feel, harmony, melody, drums, bass, instrumentation, form, and
production. The examples are reference points for listening, not templates to copy.

> Genres overlap and evolve. These are the "centre of gravity" of each style, not strict rules.

Five genres have their own deep-dive chapters: [jazz (16)](16-jazz.md), [blues (17)](17-blues.md),
[funk (18)](18-funk.md), [reggae (19)](19-reggae.md) and [heavy metal (20)](20-heavy-metal.md).

## Rock'n'roll (1950s)
- **Tempo/feel**: 140–180 BPM, **swing/shuffle** or straight 8ths, strong backbeat.
- **Harmony**: 12-bar blues, I–IV–V, dominant 7ths.
- **Melody**: blues scale, short repeated phrases, call-and-response.
- **Drums**: shuffle on snare/hat, backbeat, simple fills.
- **Bass**: upright, walking or boogie patterns (1–3–5–6).
- **Instruments**: electric guitar, piano (pounding triplets), upright bass, sax.
- **Form**: 12-bar verses with solos, short (2–2:30 min).
- **Reference**: Chuck Berry, Little Richard, Elvis, Buddy Holly.

## Blues / blues rock
- **Tempo**: slow (60–80, 12/8) to mid shuffle (100–130).
- **Harmony**: 12-bar blues with dominant 7ths, quick change, turnarounds; minor blues.
- **Melody**: minor pentatonic + blue notes **over major chords**, string bends, AAB lyrics.
- **Drums**: shuffle or slow 12/8; ride or hi-hat triplets.
- **Bass**: walking, boogie, root–fifth shuffle.
- **Instruments**: guitar (overdriven), harmonica, organ, piano.
- **Reference**: B.B. King, Muddy Waters, Cream, Stevie Ray Vaughan, Gary Moore.

## Classic rock (late 60s–70s)
- **Tempo**: 90–140 BPM, straight 8ths (some shuffle).
- **Harmony**: major keys with **♭VII and ♭III** (Mixolydian/blues), I–IV–V, power chords
  and open chords; riff-based.
- **Melody**: pentatonic, blues-based vocal, guitar solos.
- **Drums**: basic rock beat, big fills, crash-heavy choruses.
- **Bass**: roots/fifths, 8ths, doubles riffs.
- **Instruments**: 2 guitars (rhythm + lead), bass, drums, organ/piano.
- **Form**: verse–chorus with guitar solo; riff intros/outros.
- **Production**: live-sounding, roomy drums, overdriven amps.
- **Reference**: Led Zeppelin, The Rolling Stones, AC/DC, Lynyrd Skynyrd, Queen.

## Hard rock / heavy metal
- **Tempo**: 100–200+ BPM.
- **Harmony**: **power chords**, Aeolian/Phrygian minor, ♭II, ♭VI–♭VII–i, tritone,
  chromatic riffs, little functional harmony; drop-D or down-tuned guitars.
- **Melody**: riff-driven; vocals range from melodic belting (heavy metal) to harsh (extreme
  metal); harmonised guitar leads (thirds), fast virtuosic solos.
- **Drums**: double-kick 16ths, gallops (8th + two 16ths: Iron Maiden), blast beats
  (extreme), half-time breakdowns.
- **Bass**: doubles guitar riffs.
- **Instruments**: high-gain guitars (often 2+), bass, drums; keys in symphonic/power metal.
- **Dynamics**: loud throughout; contrast via clean intros/bridges and **breakdowns**.
- **Reference**: Black Sabbath, Iron Maiden, Metallica, Slayer, Pantera, Gojira.

## Punk / pop-punk
- **Tempo**: 150–200+ BPM (pop-punk ~150–180).
- **Harmony**: 3–4 **power chords**, I–IV–V, I–V–vi–IV, fast chord changes.
- **Melody**: simple, shouted (punk) or bright and catchy (pop-punk), narrow range,
  gang vocals.
- **Drums**: fast 8ths, **snare on every "&" (punk beat / "skate beat")**, double-time
  choruses, crash/ride on every beat.
- **Bass**: root 8ths, picked.
- **Instruments**: distorted guitars (downstroke 8ths, palm-muting in verses), bass, drums.
- **Form**: short (2–3 min), verse–chorus, little or no solo.
- **Dynamics**: verses palm-muted, choruses open strummed.
- **Reference**: Ramones, Sex Pistols, Green Day, Blink-182, Paramore.

## Grunge / alternative rock (90s)
- **Tempo**: 90–130 BPM.
- **Harmony**: power chords with unusual root movements (chromatic, ♭III, ♭VI), drop-D,
  minor modes.
- **Melody**: melodic but brooding, sometimes droning.
- **Signature**: **loud–quiet–loud dynamics** — clean, quiet verses vs. explosive
  distorted choruses (inherited from Pixies).
- **Drums**: heavy, powerful, simple.
- **Reference**: Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden, Pixies, Radiohead (*The Bends*).

## Indie rock / Britpop
- **Tempo**: 100–150 BPM.
- **Harmony**: diatonic with borrowed iv and ♭VII, sus/add9 chords, open-string jangly
  voicings, I–V–vi–IV with twists.
- **Melody**: singalong anthemic choruses (Britpop) or quirky, understated vocals (indie).
- **Drums**: driving straight 8ths, four-on-the-floor in dance-punk/indie-disco
  (Franz Ferdinand, The Killers), tambourine.
- **Guitars**: jangly clean (Rickenbacker), chorus/delay effects, angular riffs.
- **Reference**: Oasis, Blur, The Smiths, The Strokes, Arctic Monkeys, The Killers.

## Mainstream pop
- **Tempo**: 90–130 BPM (dance-pop 115–128).
- **Harmony**: 4-chord loops (**I–V–vi–IV, vi–IV–I–V**), often the same loop in every
  section with arrangement providing contrast; sus2/add9 colours.
- **Melody**: **the priority**. Highly repetitive hooks, pentatonic, rhythmic "melodic
  math" (lines of matching syllable counts), post-chorus hooks, wide-range chorus.
- **Drums**: programmed; four-on-the-floor or half-time; claps/snaps on the backbeat;
  trap-style hats in recent pop.
- **Bass**: synth bass, kick-locked, sidechained.
- **Instruments**: synths, programmed drums, piano/guitar for organic colour,
  vocal chops.
- **Form**: intro short, chorus by ~0:45, pre-chorus + post-chorus, bridge/breakdown,
  ~3 min total.
- **Production**: polished, compressed, layered vocals, effects-heavy transitions.
- **Reference**: Max Martin productions, Taylor Swift, Dua Lipa, The Weeknd, Ariana Grande.

## Pop-rock / power pop
- Pop melodies and form + rock instrumentation (guitars, live drums).
- 110–150 BPM, I–V–vi–IV and I–IV–V, big chorus with stacked harmonies,
  crunchy (not heavy) guitars, palm-muted verses.
- **Reference**: The Beatles (mid-period), Cheap Trick, Weezer, Kelly Clarkson, Fall Out Boy.

## Ballad / power ballad
- **Tempo**: 60–80 BPM (often 6/8 or 12/8, or 4/4 with half-time feel).
- **Harmony**: richer chords (add9, maj7, slash chords), **descending bass lines**
  (I–V/7–vi–vi/5–IV), secondary dominants, deceptive cadences.
- **Melody**: wide range, long notes, emotional climax with appoggiaturas, key change in the
  last chorus.
- **Arrangement**: starts sparse (piano or acoustic guitar + vocal), builds by adding drums
  in the 2nd verse/chorus, strings, full band and big guitar solo in power ballads.
- **Reference**: Adele, Whitney Houston, Aerosmith "I Don't Want to Miss a Thing",
  Bon Jovi, Guns N' Roses "November Rain".

## Motown / soul / R&B (classic)
- **Tempo**: 90–130 BPM.
- **Harmony**: 7th chords, I–vi–ii–V, gospel progressions (IV–iv–I, I–IV/I), secondary
  dominants, chromatic movement.
- **Melody**: gospel-derived, melismatic, call-and-response with backing vocals.
- **Drums**: four-on-the-floor snare/tambourine backbeats (Motown), tight pockets.
- **Bass**: melodic, syncopated, chromatic approaches (James Jamerson).
- **Instruments**: horns, strings, piano, organ, backing vocals, tambourine.
- **Reference**: The Supremes, Marvin Gaye, Aretha Franklin, Stevie Wonder.

## Funk
- **Tempo**: 95–120 BPM.
- **Harmony**: **one- or two-chord vamps**, dominant 9th/13th, minor 7th (Dorian), little
  harmonic movement; harmony is rhythmic.
- **Melody**: rhythmic, chant-like vocals, horn riffs.
- **Drums**: 16th-note grooves, ghost notes, strong emphasis on **"the one"**, syncopated kick.
- **Bass**: busy 16th syncopation, slap/pop, octaves, rests are as important as notes.
- **Guitar**: "chicken scratch" 16th muted strums, short chord stabs (9th chords).
- **Reference**: James Brown, Sly & the Family Stone, Parliament-Funkadelic, Prince,
  Vulfpeck.

## Disco / nu-disco
- **Tempo**: 110–130 BPM.
- **Signature**: **four-on-the-floor kick + open hi-hat on the offbeats**, octave bass,
  strings, funky guitar scratches.
- **Harmony**: minor 7th / major 7th loops, ii–V movement, Dorian.
- **Reference**: Chic, Bee Gees, Donna Summer, Daft Punk "Get Lucky", Dua Lipa *Future
  Nostalgia*.

## Reggae / ska
- **Tempo**: reggae 60–90 BPM (often felt half-time); ska 140–180 BPM.
- **Signature**: **offbeat chord "skank"** (guitar/keys on the "&" of each beat),
  one-drop drums (kick+snare on 3), deep melodic bass with space.
- **Harmony**: simple (I–IV, i–♭VII, two-chord vamps).
- **Reference**: Bob Marley, Toots & the Maytals, The Specials, The Police (reggae-rock).

## Country / folk-rock / Americana
- **Tempo**: 80–140 BPM; "train beat" drumming, shuffles, waltzes (3/4).
- **Harmony**: I–IV–V, I–vi–IV–V, simple diatonic; major pentatonic melodies.
- **Bass**: root–fifth alternating ("boom-chick").
- **Instruments**: acoustic guitar, pedal steel, fiddle, banjo, mandolin.
- **Lyrics**: narrative storytelling.
- **Reference**: Johnny Cash, Dolly Parton, Tom Petty, Fleetwood Mac, Mumford & Sons.

## Synth-pop / new wave (80s) and synthwave
- **Tempo**: 110–130 BPM.
- **Signature**: synth arpeggios and sequenced 8th/16th bass, gated reverb drums, drum
  machines (LinnDrum, TR-808/909), minor keys with bright melodies.
- **Harmony**: Aeolian loops (i–♭VI–♭VII), I–V–vi–IV, pedal bass under changing chords.
- **Reference**: Depeche Mode, a-ha, Eurythmics, New Order, The Weeknd "Blinding Lights".

## EDM-pop / dance-pop
- **Tempo**: 115–130 BPM (house), 128 typical; future bass ~140–160 (half-time).
- **Form**: intro – verse – **build** – **drop** (instrumental hook) – verse – build – drop
  – breakdown – build – drop – outro.
- **Signature**: risers, snare rolls, filter sweeps, sidechain "pumping", vocal chops,
  supersaws, four-on-the-floor.
- **Harmony**: 4-chord minor loops (vi–IV–I–V), held for the whole song.
- **Reference**: Avicii, Calvin Harris, David Guetta, Zedd, The Chainsmokers.

## Hip-hop / trap influence in pop
- **Tempo**: 70–100 BPM (boom-bap ~85–95), trap 130–160 BPM in half-time (felt 65–80).
- **Signature**: 808 sub bass (long, pitched, glides), hi-hat rolls (triplets, 32nds),
  snare/clap on 3 (half-time), sparse harmony (one loop, often minor, sampled).
- **Melody**: rhythmic vocals, sung hooks over rap verses.
- **Reference**: Kanye West, Drake, Post Malone, Billie Eilish (minimal pop-trap hybrid).

## For the pipeline

Encode each style as a **style profile** that parameterises every generator:

```yaml
# styles/pop_rock.yaml (example)
name: pop_rock
tempo: {min: 110, max: 150, default: 128}
meter: "4/4"
feel: {swing: 0.5, half_time_sections: [bridge]}
keys: {preferred: [E, A, D, G, C, B], modes: {ionian: 0.7, aeolian: 0.2, mixolydian: 0.1}}
harmony:
  templates: {axis: 0.35, doowop: 0.1, three: 0.2, mixo: 0.15, axis_vi: 0.2}
  vocabulary: [maj, min, 5, sus2, sus4, add9]
  harmonic_rhythm_bars: {verse: 1, chorus: 1, prechorus: 0.5}
form: pop_standard
drums:
  patterns: {verse: rock_basic, chorus: rock_16th_hats, bridge: half_time}
  fill_every_bars: 8
  humanize: {timing_ms: 8, velocity: 8, snare_behind_ms: 5}
bass:
  patterns: {verse: roots_whole, prechorus: eighths_pedal, chorus: eighths_driving}
  approach_prob: 0.4
instruments: [drums, bass, rhythm_guitar, lead_guitar, keys, vocal]
melody:
  scale_bias: major_pentatonic
  chorus_register_shift: +4   # semitones above verse center
```

Style is the **highest-leverage parameter**: given the same chord progression and melody,
switching the style profile should change drums, bass, voicings, tempo and arrangement
enough to produce a convincingly different song.
